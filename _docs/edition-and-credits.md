# About This English Edition

*The World as Will and Representation, Volume I*<br>
Arthur Schopenhauer<br>
First English release, v1.0.0 · September 30, 2026

English translation by **[Ratanajaya](https://github.com/ratanajaya)**, with AI assistance from **OpenAI GPT-5.5** and **OpenAI GPT-6 Sol**. Published by Ratanajaya.

This translation began with my own wish to read Schopenhauer more readily. I have great respect for E. F. J. Payne's translation and the care it brings to Schopenhauer's philosophy. As a non-native English speaker, however, I personally find its language difficult to follow. That experience inspired this contemporary English version: I wanted to help more people find their way into *The World as Will and Representation*, Schopenhauer's most famous work, and engage with its ideas. My aim is to offer an accessible entry point while preserving the depth and precision of his thought.

This edition contains the four books, §§1–71, the prefaces, and the appendix *Critique of Kantian Philosophy*. It follows the German text edited by Eduard Grisebach, *Die Welt als Wille und Vorstellung*, Band I, second corrected printing (Leipzig: Philipp Reclam jun., 1892). The historical title page and editor's remarks are retained; their publisher and edition statements describe the German source, not this English release.

The translation aims at clear modern English while retaining Schopenhauer's arguments and philosophical distinctions. Non-German quotations remain beside English renderings. Identifiable extraction errors in the flagged quotations have been corrected against published witnesses. Greek is normalized to standard letter forms and Unicode; verified passages use polytonic spelling. Historical quotation variants are preserved where replacing them would silently change Schopenhauer's citation. This is a reading edition, not a new critical edition of the German or ancient texts.

Editorial corrections were checked against the [published 1909 Haldane–Kemp Volume I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38427/38427-h/38427-h.html), its [Kant appendix in Volume II](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/40097/40097-h/40097-h.html), Greek texts in the [Perseus Digital Library](https://github.com/PerseusDL/canonical-greekLit), and Alcinous *Didaskalikos* 9.2 in George Boys-Stones's [Greek and Latin source collection](https://drupal-s3fs-prod.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/resources/academic/1415/1067/1461/Platonist_Philosophy_Texts.pdf). These are collation witnesses; this translation's English prose has not been replaced by the earlier English translation. A detailed correction record is available in the [project repository](https://github.com/ratanajaya/SchopenhauerTranslation).

## Historical Context

Schopenhauer's polemics include antisemitic descriptions and other derogatory generalizations about peoples and religions. They reflect the author's prejudices and historical setting. They are retained to represent the work accurately; their inclusion does not endorse them. In particular, the wording in §48 is Schopenhauer's, not an editorial characterization.
